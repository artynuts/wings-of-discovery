🧠 Our AI Image Detection Model

We built a machine learning model trained specifically to identify archaeological artifacts found during field walking. Our model can recognize 10 different types of artifacts, from kitchen tools to ancient currencies. It was trained using Google's Teachable Machine platform with thousands of high-quality artifact images.

🏗️ How We Built Our Model

  1. Created 10 artifact classes: Kitchen tools, Jewelry, Ceramics, Shells & Bones, Glass, Polished Gems & Rocks, Unpolished Gems & Rocks, Currencies, Hunting Tools, and Recreation items
  2. Collected training images: We photographed each artifact type from multiple angles and under different lighting conditions to ensure the model could recognize items in varied field conditions
  3. Trained the model: Using Google Teachable Machine, we trained the neural network to recognize patterns specific to drone imagery
  4. Tested and refined: The model was tested with images captured by our drone to ensure accuracy in real-world field walking scenarios

💻 Technology & Training Process

  • Google Teachable Machine: A free, user-friendly platform that made it easy to create and train our custom model without deep ML expertise
  • TensorFlow.js: Powers the model and runs the AI analysis directly in your browser
  • Neural Networks: Deep learning technology trained on thousands of artifact images to recognize visual patterns
  • Drone Imagery: Our training dataset includes images captured from our Tello drone to ensure the model works with aerial perspectives

📊 Understanding Confidence Scores

When our model analyzes an image, it provides a confidence score for each of the 10 artifact categories. These scores indicate how certain the model is about the identification:

  • 80-100%: High confidence - likely a correct identification
  • 50-80%: Medium confidence - could be correct but worth verifying
  • 0-50%: Low confidence - probably not this artifact type

Important: Always verify results with an expert archaeologist. Our model is a helpful tool for initial classification, but human expertise is essential for accurate field archaeology.

💡 Tips for Best Results

  • Lighting matters: Our model was trained with artifacts in various lighting conditions. Avoid extreme shadows or glare
  • Clean backgrounds: Simple backgrounds (like the ground or a neutral surface) help the model focus on the artifact
  • Artifact visibility: Make sure the artifact is in focus and clearly visible in the image
  • Multiple angles: When possible, capture the artifact from different angles for more accurate identification
  • Drone perspective: Images taken from drone altitude (our training scenario) may have the best results

🔒 Privacy & Security

Your images stay on your device. When you use our artifact detector, nothing is uploaded to external servers. All image analysis happens locally in your browser using TensorFlow.js. This ensures your field data remains private and secure.
