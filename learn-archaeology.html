Archaeology is the study of people from the past by looking at things they left behind—called artifacts.

Archaeologists learn about:

  • How people lived
  • What they ate
  • What tools they used
  • How they built homes and cities
✨ Archaeology helps us understand human history and protect it for the future.

Artifacts are objects made or used by humans.

Examples include:

  • Pottery pieces
  • Coins
  • Tools
  • Jewelry
  • Bones
  • Old building materials

Fieldwalking is a method archaeologists use to find artifacts without digging.

How it works:

  1. Archaeologists walk across land in straight lines
  2. They look carefully at the ground
  3. They record what they find and where they find it

This helps archaeologists:

  • Discover new sites
  • Decide where to dig
  • Protect important areas

Archaeologists use:

  • 📍 GPS coordinates
  • 🗺 Maps
  • 📸 Photos
  • 📝 Notes and drawings
Recording exact locations is very important so history is not lost.

Today, archaeologists use technology to work faster and protect sites.

Some tools include:

  • Drones
  • Satellites
  • Ground-penetrating radar
  • AI and image recognition

💡 Archaeologists do NOT study dinosaurs (that's paleontology!)
💡 Broken artifacts can be just as valuable as whole ones
💡 Archaeology is about careful thinking, not treasure hunting
💡 Kids can become archaeologists too!
